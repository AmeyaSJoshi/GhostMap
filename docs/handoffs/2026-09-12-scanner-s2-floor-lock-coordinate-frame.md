## The coordinate frame

Built exactly as `GhostCoordinateFrame`'s own documentation specifies, which is
the shared contract:

```text
up      = Vector3.up
forward = ProjectOnPlane(camera.forward, up).normalized
right   = Cross(up, forward).normalized
origin  = floor raycast hit (world space)
```

`Cross(up, forward)` is +X in Unity's left-handed basis —
`Cross((0,1,0),(0,0,1)) == (1,0,0)` — so the axes satisfy
`Cross(right, up) == forward`, the same relationship Unity's world axes satisfy.
Reversing the cross would mirror every captured room, and **nothing downstream
would notice**: area, wall length, closure error and interior angle are all
unchanged by a reflection. That is why handedness is asserted directly rather
than inferred.

The frame is immutable. `GhostCoordinateFrame` has no setters, and
`FloorLockController` refuses a second lock, so no later camera motion, plane
refinement or XR Origin change can move the origin out from under coordinates
already captured against it.

## The CameraYOffset question, answered

`docs/status/scanner.md` deferred `XROrigin.m_CameraYOffset = 1.1176` to "when
the floor is locked". It is resolved here: **it needs no compensation, and
compensating for it would be a bug.**

The concern was reasonable. The AR camera is a child of Camera Offset, whose
local Y is `CameraYOffset` because ARKit reports
`TrackingOriginModeFlags.Device`. Detected planes are not children of Camera
Offset — they hang off `XROrigin.TrackablesParent`. It looks as though the
camera and the planes sit in two spaces about 1.12 m apart.

They do not. From `com.unity.xr.core-utils@a8b9003/Runtime/XROrigin.cs`:

- line 542: `TrackablesParent` is created as a child of the XR Origin root at
  local identity;
- line 611: `OnBeforeRender` assigns `TrackablesParent.position` the pose
  returned by `GetCameraOriginPose()`;
- line 585: `GetCameraOriginPose()` returns `m_Camera.transform.parent`'s world
  pose — that is Camera Offset itself.

So the same offset is baked into both. A raycast hit and the camera position can
be subtracted from each other directly, and the frame's origin — the floor hit —
subtracts the shared offset away exactly. The floor lands on Ghost `y = 0` for
any offset value, and the camera reads its true eye height above the floor.

Two tests pin this down (`CameraYOffsetDoesNotMoveTheNormalizedFloor`,
`FloorStaysAtGhostZeroForAnyCameraYOffset`), and a mutation that "compensates"
by subtracting 1.1176 from the origin fails 8 tests.

**The rule this depends on: read world space only.** Mixing in a *local*
position — the camera's `localPosition`, which the S1 diagnostics print as
"Cam L" — would reintroduce the offset. Nothing in `ArSpatialProvider` reads one.

## Assumptions and deliberate deviations

1. **`TryGetFloorHit` returns a `FloorHit`, not an `ARRaycastHit`.** The
   implementation plan sketches `out ARRaycastHit hit`. `ARRaycastHit` cannot be
   constructed in an EditMode test — it needs a live `ARPlane` trackable owned by
   a running plane subsystem — so that signature would make `ISpatialProvider`
   unmockable and push every floor-lock rule onto a phone to verify. `FloorHit`
   carries the world position, the plane alignment and the trackable id, which is
   everything the lock uses.

2. **Only `PlaneAlignment.HorizontalUp` may be locked.** Vertical planes and
   ceilings (`HorizontalDown`) are rejected. The plan says "must hit horizontal
   plane"; a ceiling is horizontal too, and locking one would put the origin
   above the user's head and invert which way corners lie.

3. **A near-vertical camera aim is rejected** (`DegenerateForward`). Projecting a
   straight-down forward onto the floor leaves rounding noise, and normalizing
   that would set the room's yaw arbitrarily. The threshold is
   `MinForwardProjectionMagnitude = 1e-3`, which only rejects an aim within about
   0.06 degrees of straight down. A 70-degree downward aim still locks, and there
   is a test for that so the guard cannot quietly tighten.

4. **The full phase transition table is encoded, but S2 only drives it to
   `FloorLocked`.** The table comes verbatim from implementation plan section 10.
   Encoding it now costs about twenty lines and makes an illegal transition throw
   immediately instead of producing a snapshot with an impossible `scanPhase`.

5. **Degraded tracking does not walk the phase backwards.** The plan defines no
   `FindFloor -> WaitingForTracking` transition, so none was invented. Capture is
   gated by `FloorLockController.CanLock` instead, which disables the button.

6. **No ray/floor capture method was added to the runtime.** Intersecting the
   camera ray with the locked floor belongs to Task S3. The required intersection
   tests verify the composition S2 delivers — `frame.WorldRayToGhost` feeding
   `RayPlaneMath.TryIntersectHorizontalPlane` — without pre-building S3.

7. **`EventSystem` uses `InputSystemUIInputModule`, not
   `StandaloneInputModule`.** The legacy module reads `UnityEngine.Input`, which
   is precisely the layer that was dead in the second S1 device failure. This is
   the first interactive UI in the project, so the EventSystem is new; a missing
   or wrongly-wired one would present on device as a button that silently does
   nothing, which is why `ScannerSceneBuilder.VerifyScene()` asserts it.

## Physical-device test procedure

### What you should see on launch
The S1 diagnostics block at the top, unchanged. Below it, near the bottom:

```text
Phase: <phase> | rev <n>
Session: <state> | reason: <reason>
Can lock: YES/no [| last attempt: <rejection>]
Crosshair: <alignment> W(x,y,z) [G(x,y,z)]
Frame: not locked
Cam W(x,y,z) [G(x,y,z)]
```

A round reticle sits dead center, and a "Lock Floor" button sits above the
bottom edge.

### Step 1 — reach FindFloor
Stand in the middle of the room holding the phone normally. Sweep it slowly
across the floor for a few seconds so ARKit finds planes.

- `Phase` goes `Boot` -> `WaitingForTracking` -> `FindFloor`.
- `Session: SessionTracking`, `reason: None`.
- The reticle turns from grey to amber; the Lock Floor button becomes tappable.

### Step 2 — aim
Point the crosshair at a clear patch of floor **about one to two metres ahead of
your feet**, not straight down. `Crosshair:` should read `HorizontalUp` with a
world position. Note roughly where that spot is on the floor — you will aim at
it again later.

### Step 3 — lock
Tap **Lock Floor**.

- `Phase` becomes `FloorLocked` and `rev` becomes `1`.
- The reticle turns green and the button greys out.
- `Frame O W(...)` and `Frame X(...) Z(...)` appear.
- `Handedness: +1.000`.

### Step 4 — verify the frame holds while you move
Walk around the room, turn on the spot, crouch, stand, and aim at different
walls for at least thirty seconds.

### What must change
- `Cam W(...)` and `Cam G(...)` both change continuously.
- `Cam G` y is your camera height above the floor, roughly 1.3–1.7 m held at
  chest or eye level. It should drop when you crouch and rise when you stand.
- `Cam G` x and z change as you walk: walking in the direction you were facing
  when you locked increases z; stepping to your right at that moment increases x.
- `Crosshair: ... G(...)` changes as you sweep the floor, and its **y stays
  within about ±0.03 of 0** everywhere on the real floor.

### What must stay fixed
- `Frame O W(...)` — every digit. It must not move at all.
- `Frame X(...)` and `Frame Z(...)` — every digit.
- `Handedness: +1.000`.
- `Phase: FloorLocked`, `rev 1`.

### Step 5 — return to the locked spot
Aim the crosshair back at the patch of floor you locked. `Crosshair: ... G`
should read close to `(0.00, 0.00, 0.00)`.

### Tolerances that indicate success
| Reading | Expected |
| --- | --- |
| `Handedness` | exactly `+1.000` |
| `Frame O`, `Frame X`, `Frame Z` | bit-identical for the whole session |
| Crosshair ghost y anywhere on the floor | within ±0.03 m of 0 |
| Ghost coords back at the locked spot | within ±0.10 m of (0,0,0) |
| `Cam G` y standing | 1.2–1.8 m, and it tracks crouching |
| Walking 2 m forward from the lock aim | `Cam G` z increases by roughly 2 |
| Stepping 1 m right | `Cam G` x increases by roughly 1 |

Small drift over minutes is ARKit, not the frame. A sudden jump is not.

### Failure symptoms to report back
- The button never becomes tappable -> report `Session` and `reason`.
- Tapping does nothing at all and `last attempt:` never appears -> the
  EventSystem or button wiring; report that no rejection text appeared.
- `last attempt:` shows a rejection -> report **which**
  (`PlaneNotHorizontalUp`, `NoFloorHit`, `TrackingNotGood`, `DegenerateForward`).
- `Handedness` is `-1.000` -> the frame is mirrored.
- Crosshair ghost y is a consistent non-zero on the floor (especially near
  ±1.12) -> a space mix-up; report the value and `Frame O`.
- `Frame O` changes after locking -> the frame is not immutable in practice.
- `Cam G` y is about 1.12 m too high or too low -> the camera offset is not
  cancelling; report `Cam W` y, `Frame O` y and `Offset W` from the S1 block.
- Walking forward decreases z, or right decreases x -> an axis sign error.
